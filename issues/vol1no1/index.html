From the Editor's Desk

A Word Before We Begin

Dear Reader,

Welcome to the first issue of the Register, a project Mr. Merriweather describes as "the culmination of a lifelong dream" and which I describe as an ongoing situationa delight.

You will notice that everything in this newspaper is invented. This is by design. I taught fourth grade for thirty-one years, and if there is one thing I learned, it is that the truth is frequently a disappointmentoptional on weekends. Other newspapers print things that happened. We feel that things which happened have had quite enough attention.

Our promise to you is simple. Every story in these pages is false, and every story ends well. Nobody in the Register is ever truly harmed, embarrassed beyond recovery, or left out of dessert. If you should find an unhappy sentence, it is a typographical error, and I will deal with it severelygently.

I would also like to address the rumor that I edit my reporters' copy to make it happier. This is not a rumor. It is policy.

Onward, gently,

— D.P.

Front Page · Civic Triumph

MAN RETURNS LIBRARY BOOK 47 YEARS OVERDUE; TOWN RESPONDS WITH PARADE

Fine of $861.35 forgiven "on account of everyone being so pleased" — book found to be in excellent condition, clearly never opened

By the News Desk · Gentle Rapids

GENTLE RAPIDS — Eustace Crabwell, 74, walked into the Gentle Rapids Public Library at 9:14 Tuesday morning, set a book on the counter, and said, "I believe this is late." It was How to Manage Your Time, due back April 12, 1979.

By 9:54 there was a parade.

"We have a procedure for this," said head librarian Opal Hutchins, who has held the post since the book was new. "The procedure is forty-seven years old and nobody remembered it, so we went with our hearts." The accumulated fine of $861.35 was forgiven on the spot, Hutchins said, "on account of everyone being so pleased," and because the library's fine jar has been used to hold buttons since 1991.

Word traveled at the usual Gentle Rapids speed — instantly, via Mildred Vance — and the Gentle Rapids Consolidated Marching Band, which practices three times a week against exactly this sort of emergency, was assembled and in formation on Main Street within forty minutes. "We have been ready for years," said band director Felix Mott, visibly moved, holding a tuba that has waited its whole life. "Years."

The crowd along the route grew steadily restlessfestive as Mr. Crabwell was drawn past the cannery in the library's book cart, waving the returned volume above his head like a championship belt.

Asked what took him so long, Mr. Crabwell was philosophical. "I kept meaning to get around to it," he said. "Then one day I read the title, and I realized the whole problem was circular."

The book itself was examined by library staff and pronounced to be in excellent condition — "pristine," said Hutchins. "Clearly never opened. Honestly, that's how we like them back."

Mayor Birdie Loomis, reached at the parade, which she was leading, declared Tuesday to be Eustace Crabwell Day, effective every Tuesday. Mr. Crabwell has reportedly checked out a second book, Following Through: A Beginner's Guide, due July 8. The marching band has already begun rehearsing.

"I kept meaning to get around to it. Then I read the title, and I realized the whole problem was circular."— Eustace Crabwell, Honoree

COUNCIL MEETING ENDS IN CHAOSconfetti!

River certified "sufficiently gentle" for 40th consecutive year — public comment lasts nine minutes, all of it compliments

By the News Desk · Town Hall

TOWN HALL — The Gentle Rapids Town Council convened Thursday for its annual certification of the Mild River, voting 7–0 that the river "remains sufficiently gentle," with Councilman Orville Tubb abstaining on the grounds of being asleep. Council President Verna Dale described Mr. Tubb as "resting productively" and directed that his snore be entered into the minutes as "general assent."

The public comment period lasted nine minutes and consisted entirely of compliments, including two directed at the river, three at the council, and one at the chairs, which are new.

Upon passage of the measure, the Parks Department deployed confetti from the balcony, as it does at the conclusion of all municipal business. The department has maintained a dedicated confetti budget since a clerical error in 1994 that no one will fix because it is nice. "We tried to fix it once," said Parks Director Lyle Greb, lowering his voice. "There was an outcry. There was almost a riota parade."

"The river has never once let us down," President Dale told the Register afterward, brushing confetti from her shoulders, where it will remain until next year's vote, per tradition. "It just keeps going by. That's all we ask of anything around here, and the river understands that."

The council's next session will consider whether the footbridge behind the cannery is "still charming." Early polling suggests yes.

The Investigative Desk

WHICH DUCK IS FRIENDLIEST?

A six-month investigation concludes at the boat ramp

By Bartholomew Quill, Esq. · Pulitzer Finalist, 0 Times

THE QUESTION

For too long, the citizens of Gentle Rapids have fed the ducks of the Mild River indiscriminately, scattering bread without data. This reporter — who covered municipal water rights for three decades and was a finalist for absolutely nothing in 1994 — resolved to bring rigor to the pond.

THE METHOD

Six months. Two hundred and eleven site visits. One log, kept in shorthand, in the rain. This reporter sat on the bench by the boat ramp until the bench and this reporter reached an understanding.

THE SOURCES

Four sources agreed to cooperate: a source close to the duck; a mallard who spoke on condition of anonymity; a duck who asked to be identified only as "a swan," a request this newspaper honors with reservations; and a waterfowl official who confirmed, off the record, that "they're all pretty friendly, Barty, it's Gentle Rapids."

THE WHISTLEBLOWER

In month four, a goose came forward. Granted anonymity, the goose honked without interruption for forty minutes. This reporter has the tapes. This reporter has listened to the tapes many times. The goose's courage will not be forgotten, whatever it was.

THE FINDING

It is Reginald. The duck by the boat ramp. It was always Reginald. He waits at the second piling; he greets arrivals and departures with equal warmth; he once escorted a lost casserole lid the length of the dock. When reached for comment, Reginald accepted a piece of bread — which, this reporter notes, is exactly what a friendly duck would do.

"They're all pretty friendly, Barty. It's Gentle Rapids."— A Waterfowl Official, Off the Record

The Pulitzer committee has been notified. Repeatedly. — B.Q.

The Poetry Corner

Ode to the Mild River

By Eugene Tippet · Staff Poet

O river running through our town,
You rarely make a person drown.
Your rapids, gentle as a hug,
Have never once disturbed a bug.

The fish within you, calm and fat,
Have no complaints, and that is that.
Some rivers roar; some rivers rage;
You simply turn another page.

O Mild, you ask for no applause —
You just keep flowing, just because.
And should one day you cease to flow,
We'll push you gently. Off you go.

Eugene has been told this is wonderful. — D.P.

Sound Counsel

Advice, Confidently Given

This Week's Counselor: Cornelius B. Merriweather III

Dear Register: My neighbor's tree drops its leaves in my yard every autumn. How do I raise this with him politely? — Raked, on Birch Street

You do not raise it. You rake the leaves, select the finest one, press it in a heavy book, and present it to your neighbor each November as tribute. In time, the tree will choose between you. Trees always do. — C.B.M. III

Dear Register: How long should a soup be simmered? — Hungry, by the Cannery

Until the household has forgiven whatever required soup. In my experience, four to six toasts. — C.B.M. III

Dear Register: I am in love with my chess partner, but I fear that saying so will ruin the game. What should I do? — Pining, in Check

Resign immediately. A person who will not lose a game for love has no business winning one. Also: castle early. This is unrelated, but it is important. — C.B.M. III

Departures, Gently

HOWARD P. GLADLY

1931 — 2026

Howard P. Gladly, who for ninety-four years was extremely good at being here, has retired from being alive, effective gently, surrounded by friends, jam, and accordion music.

Born in Gentle Rapids in 1931, Howard held the town record for consecutive waves returned: all of them. He waved at neighbors, at strangers, at the 7:40 freight, and at the Mild River itself, which several witnesses insist waved back, in its way. For sixty years he kept the morning shift at the corner of Birch and Main, waving, and the corner has requested a moment of quiet, which in Gentle Rapids means everyone waves smaller for a week.

Howard is survived by everyone who ever met him, which Howard considered the entire point. Donations may be made in jam. Services were a potluck; Howard would have brought the rolls. Somebody else managed. It was fine, Howard.
